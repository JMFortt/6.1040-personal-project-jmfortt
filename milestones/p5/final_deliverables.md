## Study Reports

### Study Report 1

User 1 (designated M for simplicity) began the user study by logging in easily, before explicitly instructed to do so. It seems like that setup is sufficiently intuitive. She did not notice that recovery options were not listed. Thus, task 1 was completed. On task 2, M noticed immediately that shifts were greyed out and correctly recognized what that meant, but got confused by the shifts that were not greyed out because they did not list her username first (as there were others on the shift that happened to appear first in the list). She initially took this to mean that the app was bugged until further investigation (clicking) made it clear to her which shifts were hers. On task 3, M began attempting to request shift cover by clicking around on the master schedule, before noticing different published weeks. She was able to quickly succeed in her task after that hurdle. On task 4, M was immediately able to select the attendance tab and understand the markings (this seemed very clear and almost immediate). For task 5, M marked current attendance easily via the attendance page, but got overwhelmed when looking for an unspecified shift. Found it after a moment when given a specific day. On task 6, M found the analytics page quickly and correctly identified the staff member with the most chronic absences. Overall, this user liked the concept and thought it was easy to use. She thought it was intuitive, liked the color scheme, and thought the analytics page was "cute." She did point out that finding a specific absence, etc. without knowing which date it was from was difficult, which I could tell by watching her attempt it. I was happy she seemed to like the app, though I think her initial confusion on task 2 is another area that could be improved.

### Study Report 2

User 2 (designated S for simplicity) began the user study by easily logging in, so task 1 was completed easily. She did not mention the lack of recovery options explicitly either. On task 2, she quickly noticed that multiple people were on a shift and identified hers by noting which ones were not greyed-out. On task 3, she first went to the cover requests page, then the master schedule, and then found the correct place to request covers (published weeks) but took a moment to recognize which week was current. For task 4, S found the attendance immediately and understood the markings just as fast. Didn't seem to find them difficult to understand at all. On task 5, S found and marked the current attendance easily but got turned around trying to search through specific shifts. In the process, she got confused whether the attendance page was for her or someone else's attendance. Eventually found the correct shift when given a specific day, but took a moment to cycle through the shifts on that day while searching. On task 6, S began by correctly realizing that the attendance page was her attendance, then checked the members page. Eventually we both realized that the analytics page wasn't showing (unlike for user 1) until refreshing the page. After refreshing, she easily found the analytics page and chronically-absent Tim. Overall, S really liked the app and the simple layout. She, similar to user 1, found the shortened names on shift squares confusing, and thought that automatically going to the most recent published week would be useful. She also thought that selecting a week and then having to scroll through all of the shifts on that week was confusing when marking attendance. Pretty much everything else this user was a fan of.

## Design Flaws/Improvement Opportunities

1. Shortened names on shift slot rectangles (physical, moderate): this refers to the fact that on shift slots, users see a truncated portion of names assigned to that slot and not necessarily their own. This caused some confusion initially for users trying to identify their shifts. This could be addressed by representing the names a different way, dropping them entirely from the visual slots, or having some way to toggle views for different staff members to see what they'd see, thus eliminating the need for a list on each slot.

2. Selecting specific shifts for attendance marking by week (physical, minor): this refers to selecting a week, then a specific slot on that week when marking attendance on the attendance page. The same day can show up multiple times if there are multiple shifts, so it gets annoying to scroll through. This could be fixed by choosing days instead of weeks, or weeks and days after, and THEN specific shift slots.

3. Automatically loading the master schedule instead of published weeks (conceptual, minor): this refers to the issue users had identifying the concept of published weeks vs main schedule on first use. They found the main schedule, but didn't even think to look for specific published weeks until later. This could be fixed by making the published weeks more prominent in the UI.

4. Finding a specific marking without a specified date is difficult (physical, critical): this refers to the difficulty both users had in finding an incorrect attendance marking when not given a date. Although in theory this shouldn't be much of an issue, it was surprisingly difficult to cycle through all of the previous shifts looking for the one that was marked unexpectedly. There are a few ways to fix this, but the one that came to mind for me is similar to the proposed solution for flaw #1. Essentially: having a toggle feature where TAs/STAFF members can click to see the schedule the way that a given staff member would (with correct shift coloring). That way, when looking for an absence with an unspecified date, one could toggle that staff member on the schedule view, and click through the published weeks until they see a big red rectangle. That'd make the process much easier.
